# Three Balloons, Four Cakes, One Teary-Eyed Maa

The next little project I poured my heart into was celebrating my mother-in-law’s birthday — her 56th, and her very first one with us here in the US. Even more special? It fell on 15th April, which just so happened to be the exact day Dubu turned 3 months old. Clearly, the universe was sending us a sign to make this a day to remember.

Oh, and as if that wasn't enough, 15th April was a Tuesday — the day of the week dedicated to Hanuman Ji. We now read Hanuman Chalisa together as a family every Tuesday, in honor of Dubu’s name, Aanjaneya, another name of Hanuman Ji.

## Keeping It Simple (Because... Baby!)

Given the whole newborn life situation, the plan was to keep things super simple. I’d just returned to work one day before (because why not add another layer of chaos), and it was a weekday, with Sagar juggling his own work meetings. So we said: no pressure. Just a small dinner, a homemade cake, and something sweet to mark the day.

We planned for our all-time favorite cake recipe — a no-maida, no-sugar, date-and-walnut wonder that Sagar has totally mastered. But as the day got closer... let’s just say the plan evolved (as they always do around here).

## The Sneaky Surprise: Late-Night Decor Mission

One evening we looked at each other and said, “Why not decorate the living room? She's never had a proper birthday celebration back in India.” We decided to set everything up as a surprise, after she went to bed.

So over the weekend, Sagar and I gathered supplies: number balloons — a 5, 6, and a 3 — to represent both milestones, plus a cheerful helium balloon that read "Happy Birthday." We borrowed gold foil star balloons from a friend (because why not reuse and reduce clutter!), and hid everything away until the night before.

After convincing Maa to sleep early — mission half accomplished — we ran into a problem: Dubu would not sleep. White noise, swaying, milk, dim light — we tried it all. He finally drifted off, but left us on high alert. Any sound above a whisper and our little birthday bear would wake up.

So, we got to work — tiptoeing and whispering — trying to figure out how to arrange the balloons.

## “Dubu and Dadi Day” — The Banner That Almost Wasn’t

Now that the balloons were up, we were like — wait, it would be SO cute to have a banner that says “Dubu and Dadi Day.” Not just some generic birthday sign, but something that felt like us (honestly, the one we bought looked so tacky).

Only problem? All my art supplies were in the room where Maa was sleeping. There was no way I was sneaking in to grab glitter pens or scissors without risking waking her up. So now we had a perfect idea, but zero materials to execute it.

Enter: Sagar’s legendary stash of postcards. This man uses these postcards for every occasion. I’m not even kidding — for thank you-s, birthday messages, Diwali notes, random feelings. And as always, they rose to the occasion.

We found a neat, clean set of cards, and I grabbed a thick black marker (thankfully not locked away with the rest of the art stuff). I quickly scribbled “Dubu and Dadi” in big bold letters — minimal effort, maximum charm. And when we stuck them up on the wall? Honestly, it looked perfect.

So yes, no fancy banners. Just a last-minute DIY idea, a black marker, and Sagar’s forever-useful postcard collection. And it ended up being one of my favorite things about the whole setup.

![“Dubu and Dadi Day” — The Banner That Almost Wasn’t](images/dubu-dadi-birthday/dubu-dadi-birthday-02.jpg)

## A Footprint Card

After the decorations were up and the room was clean (yes, we cleaned even at 1:30 in the night), I felt like something was still missing on the center table. “Yaar, kuch fresh flowers hote toh kitna accha lagta,” I thought. But obviously, we weren’t about to pluck roses from thin air, and even Trader Joe’s was fast asleep. So, next best thing? A handmade card! We picked a simple, sweet design and thought — let’s add Dubu’s footprint to make it extra special.

The good news? I had the ink and stamp kit outside. The not-so-good news? Dubu was in one of those moods. He refused to cooperate. He wriggled, he pulled his foot away, he kicked the ink pad, he almost stamped our faces — basically, he stamped everywhere but the card. But we kept at it.

And after some retries (and a lot of pleading with a very sleepy baby), we managed to get one perfect stamp on the card. And just like that, our DIY centerpiece was complete: a tiny footprint, full of love.

![A Footprint Card](images/dubu-dadi-birthday/dubu-dadi-birthday-03.jpg)

## Cake x 4 (Because Why Not?)

Our original plan was Sagar’s date-walnut cake. But on a whim, we decided to try a rasmalai cake — something new, something Indian, something she hadn’t had before. We called a bunch of bakeries, and most of them said no (because Tuesday delivery + Saturday order = too little time). But thankfully, one baker said yes!

And in a moment of cake-fueled excitement, we didn’t stop at one. We also ordered three adorable jar cakes in other flavors — each one cuter and yummier than the next. They added that perfect surprise wow element to the table.

## Gifts That Took Time, Not Space

We thought a lot about what to gift her, and honestly — we were stuck. Gifting has always been a tricky thing for me. I’m someone who doesn’t believe in gifting just for the sake of it. It feels like unnecessary pressure. I’d pick a handwritten note or a small homemade card any day over some fancy decorative piece that ends up collecting dust in a house already short on space.

Of course, not everyone sees it that way — and that’s where the stress begins: what do they want, and will it actually be useful? (Underline useful, please.)

After going back and forth, we finally agreed that we wouldn’t gift a thing, but rather gift our time. Time is so much harder to give than money these days, and that’s exactly what makes it meaningful. So, we decided on two special gifts for her — both made with love, not bought from a store. One was a surprise video from her favorite people, and the other was a customized storybook that we’re still putting the final touches on.

First, the video. We reached out to her friends and family, asking them to send short clips — wishing her, sharing a memory, singing a song, or just saying what she means to them. Sagar then stitched them all together into the sweetest birthday surprise.

We played the video during lunch on Tuesday. Usually, lunch is accompanied by MasterChef or Indian Idol, but that day, we swapped it out for this. And she had no idea. There were clips from mausis, singing her favorite old songs, and a video from mama, wishing her a heartfelt happy birthday. Aadu, her grandson, wished her happy birthday the cutest way and asked her to bring Legos for him. Sagar joked about how she’s the reason he now hates shopping. Papa sang the cutest love song for her. And in the end, Sagar made a montage of her moments spent in the U.S., while he sang "Dil Hai Chhota Sa" in the background.

She had tears in her eyes. That moment — her surprise, her joy, her emotion — told us we had given her the perfect gift.

## A Storybook in the Making

The second gift — one we haven’t handed over just yet — is a customized storybook that captures her bond with Dubu. We decided to make it ourselves — complete with avatars of both of them and a simple story that unfolds across the pages, one short poem at a time.

It took a loooooot of time. Like, so much time that at one point I wondered if it would ever get done. But that’s also what makes it special. This wasn’t a plug-and-play Wonderbly kind of book (though I do love those!). This was built from scratch, with real avatars and a completely original story — not one of those pre-written templates.

That said, I’m not giving up on online books — because honestly, making a book from scratch is a whole project in itself. I’ll probably only do it on very special occasions. But this one was worth it.

Right now, it’s off getting printed — but I already know she’s going to love it.

## Friends, Food, and One Last Surprise

We had also invited a few friends over — and they were bringing cake. Again, she had absolutely no clue. In her mind, we were too busy and tired to be baking or ordering anything. Also, by lunchtime, she had already been surprised with the decorations and the video, and she looked so happy that I don’t think she minded the lack of cake one bit. We casually told her we’d just make chow mein and chilli paneer at home, since going out wasn’t really an option with Dubu right now.

She was like, “Okay, that sounds yum!”

And then — the doorbell rang. Our friends walked in with cakes (yes, four of them). Her face when she saw them? Priceless.

We had such a good time — laughing, eating, and just enjoying the company. One friend even got us paani puri! The perfect end to a perfectly imperfect little celebration.
